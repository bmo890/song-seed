The three drawers

What changed, and the rule behind it

  1. Three drawers, never two or four. The tab bar keeps its shape on every clip — a clip with no beat grid shows Click disabled with a reason rather than dropping a tab and moving the others under your thumb.
  2. Tabs hide state, so the tabs carry a dot. A drawer holding something that is on wears a small terracotta dot. Loop and Sound have one here. Collapsed, the panel keeps a one-line summary — 0.75× · loop 0:14–0:26 — so nothing has to be opened to be known.
  3. Selection replaces per-row controls. Six marks used to mean twenty-four controls and no focus. Now: one selected mark, one inspector, and the edit dialogs — including the full-spectrum hue slider — are deleted outright. Nothing tactile is lost: edge-drag, body-drag and reel scrubbing all remain, the inspector only adds precision on top — its nudge step scales with the reel's zoom, so it is a micro-adjust tool, not a second way to drag.
  4. No absolute popovers. Every disclosure pushes the content below it, which is what makes the unreachable speed slider structurally impossible rather than merely fixed.
  5. Soft keys, not stadium pills. Hide marks / Tools are radii.lg keys; the only circles left on the screen are the transport's play button and the ✕. Solid terracotta is spent once.
  6. One name per thing. Part, not section-then-part. Layer, not overdub-then-layer. Times read 0:38, matching every card elsewhere in the app.
