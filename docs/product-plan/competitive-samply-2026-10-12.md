# Competitive read: Samply, [untitled], Dubnote (2026-10-12)

The founder asked how SongNook stacks up against Samply. This is the research,
the conclusions, and a task backlog drawn from them. Nothing here is built or
scheduled. It exists so tasks can be cut from it later.

## The short answer

Samply is not a direct competitor. It is an approval tool for the end of a
record: an engineer uploads a mix, clients listen and comment, a master gets
signed off. SongNook is a capture tool for the start of a song.

The two touch at one seam: "I have a work in progress and want someone to hear
it." That seam is where SongNook is weakest today.

The closer rivals are **[untitled]** and **Dubnote**. Both are phone-first
songwriter sketchbooks, both charge, and both have sharing SongNook lacks.

## How the research was done, and its limits

- Sources: Samply's own site and pricing page, the App Store listing and
  written reviews, a third-party review tally, four competitor teardowns, press
  on [untitled] and Dubnote.
- No independent forum discussion of Samply surfaced. No Reddit threads were
  found, and the one Gearspace thread sat behind a bot wall. The critical
  picture leans on App Store reviews and rival blogs.
- Competitor blogs (Audome, Aliada, Echoe, DropCue) are sales copy. Only claims
  that recur across them are treated as believable.
- Rating figures differ by aggregator. None of these apps was tested hands-on.

## Samply

Web, macOS, iPhone. No Android app, no recording, no lyrics, no editing. A
private lossless player with versions and comments.

| Plan | Price | Storage | Adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | Free | 4 GB | Lossless gapless streaming, unlimited projects, time-coded comments |
| Indie | $5 / month | 1 TB | Unlimited versions, download prevention, passwords, listener insights, payments |
| Pro | $28 / month | 5 TB | Spatial audio, ID3 parsing, watermarking, upload portal, Zapier |
| Studio | Contact | n/a | Multi-user workspace |

- Positioning: "the best way to share audio", aimed at mix and mastering
  engineers.
- Social proof is the product's front door: named engineers (Andrew Scheps,
  Oli Jacobs, Pete Lyman, Mike Tucci) and the claim that every 2026 Record of
  the Year nominee used it.
- App Store: 5.0 from 2.9K ratings. Written reviews score lower, 4.4 from 134.
- Ships a release every one to two weeks, nearly all bug fixes.

### What users praise

| Theme | Mentions |
|---|---|

### What users complain about

| Theme | Mentions |
|---|---|
| No shuffle | 14 |

Other grievances: quit and relaunch to see a new version, playlist recipients
who cannot play some tracks, "uploading is a chore for artists", one reviewer
threatening to leave for [untitled].

Requests that matter to SongNook: **lyrics attached to a track**, and **album
or project groupings** instead of single songs. SongNook has both.

Recurring competitor claims: browser playback is re-encoded to AAC, versions
carry no notes, private listeners must create an account.

## [untitled] (the real neighbour)

- iPhone and web. Records in-app, imports by AirDrop and iMessage, lossless,
  projects and folders, non-destructive versions, offline mode, trimming,
  expiring share links, cloud sync.
- $7.99 / month or $49.99 / year, behind a hard paywall during onboarding.
- 4.9 stars. Third-party revenue estimates of $100K to $200K per month.
- Weak where SongNook is strong: no lyrics, no chords, no sections, no grid.
  Paywall before the first upload. Buried actions. An import icon that looks
  like Voice Memos but opens Files.

## Dubnote

- iOS only. On-device tempo detection, lyric transcription, automatic section
  split, notebooks shared with co-writers who can add time-stamped comments
  and their own recordings.
- $24.99 / year.

## Where Samply is ahead, and whether it matters to SongNook

| # | Samply strength | Matters to SongNook? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One sentence, one customer. Nothing in the product fights it. | Yes. SongNook has no such sentence. |
| 2 | Versions are first-class: stack, switch mid-playback at the same position, loudness matched. | Yes. Cheap to borrow for takes. |
| 3 | Feedback lands on the tape as time-coded comments. | Yes. SongNook has pins and no sender. |
| 4 | Anyone can listen from a link with no account. | Yes. The largest gap. |
| 5 | A desktop surface exists. | Later. |
| 6 | A business model. | Later. |
| 7 | Listener analytics, paywalls, watermarking, spatial audio, Zapier. | No. Different market. |

## Where SongNook is ahead

1. **Capture.** Samply cannot record. SongNook owns the first thirty seconds:
   lock-screen recording, count-in, metronome grid, pins while the tape rolls.
2. **Words against the tape.** Lyrics and charts on the timeline, autoscroll
   while recording, version stamps.
3. **Musical structure.** Sections, pins, bars and beats, practice loop,
   step-up, overdub layers.
4. **Local-first, no account.** Half of Samply's complaints are server
   weather. That class of failure cannot happen here. It is also a stronger
   trust pitch for unreleased work.
5. **Android ships.** Samply and Dubnote have none.
6. **Hebrew and RTL.**
7. **Calm.** A rival's teardown says Samply changes its UI often.

## Where SongNook is lacking

- **No feedback loop.** A non-user cannot hear a clip or answer it. All three
  neighbours fill this gap.
- **No web or desktop surface.** No link anyone can open, no drop zone for
  files from a computer.
- **Take comparison is a list, not a switch.**
- **No social proof.** No store reviews, no named users.
- **No positioning sentence.** "A quiet place to finish creative work" guides
  design. It does not tell a stranger what the app does or who it is for.
- **No monetization plan.** Acceptable for v1. The category demonstrably pays.

## Positioning and wedge

A positioning sentence says who the app is for and what it does for them. A
wedge is the one narrow person and problem won first.

- Proposed wedge: the songwriter with hundreds of voice memos named "New
  Recording 47" who finishes none of them. Voice Memos does nothing for this
  person. Samply does not touch them. [untitled] and Dubnote half do.
- Candidate sentences, founder to choose or rewrite:
  - "Where voice memos become finished songs."
  - "Record the idea. Finish the song."

Once chosen, the sentence is a test for every surface:

| Surface | Verdict against the sentence |
|---|---|
| Recorder, clips, sketches, takes, lyrics, charts, marks | Core |
| Shelf, Revisit | Supports it, brings old ideas back |
| Setlists, Compilations, Received | After the song is finished. Keep, quieter. |
| Sparks, Magpie, Word Ladder | Adjacent. Belong inside the lyric editor. |

Nothing is deleted by this. It decides what a new user meets first.

Publish a clip or sketch to a web page anyone can open and play, no account.

- [ ] Must not disturb the reel, practice loop, or step-up.

### Not doing

Listener analytics, paywalls on downloads, watermarking, spatial audio,
Zapier, shuffle. These serve Samply's market, not SongNook's.

### Keep saying

Free, no account, works offline, your recordings stay on your phone. None of
the three rivals can copy that claim.

## Open notes from the founder conversation (2026-10-12/13)

Nothing in this section is decided. It records where the thinking stands so
it is not lost.

### The share link, reconsidered

- A web listen page with comments is Samply's product. Its benefits are reach
  and acquisition only. The listener gets none of what makes SongNook worth
  using: the loop, sections, chart, pins. Those need the app, and the app is
  free with no account, so install is a small ask.
- Alternative shape under discussion:
  - Free: what Send does today. A snapshot by link, opens in SongNook, expires.
  - Premium: hosting. The material lives on the server, stays available, and
    updates when the sender changes a chord or moves a pin. This is the
    "synced" side of the wall in `sharing-and-received-architecture.md`.
  - Web page stays thin: plays the audio, shows the chart read-only, one
    "Open in SongNook" button. No comments, no web product to maintain.
- Business shape: one leader pays, four to six players install free. Every
  band is a growth loop and the need recurs every gig.
- Comments between people wait for accounts.

### Two spaces, one object

- The writing side only ever produces a sketch. SongNook is not a DAW. The
  album is finished elsewhere. Finished recordings then come back in to be
  learned. Two flows, both starting and ending outside the app, sometimes
  intersecting, sometimes not. That is fine.
- What the two spaces share is not a flow but an object: a recording with
  musical structure on it. Sketch you made, mix you were sent, rehearsal you
  taped. The tools and gestures are identical across all three. The reel is
  the product.
- Consequence for the pitch: describe the object and a few situations, not a
  user type and not a journey. "Teach it to the band" was rejected as corny
  because it narrates a hand-off that does not exist.
- Planning vocabulary only, never shown in the app: the sides are **writing**
  and **rehearsal**, the people are **writers** and **players**. Musicians'
  own words for the rehearsal side: learning the set, gig prep, the book,
  bandleader or MD, players and subs.

### Positioning line, candidates

"Working" is the preferred adjective. It does what "evolving" and "active"
try to do without announcing it, and musicians already use it: working
musician, work in progress, working tape. Hebrew: מחברת עבודה is an ordinary
phrase.

| Candidate | Note |
|---|---|
| A working notebook for musicians. | Current favourite. Plain, both sides. |
| The musician's working notebook. | More ownership, stiffer. |
| A notebook you can hear. | Says what paper cannot. Risks cute. |
| Notes you can play. | Shorter. Could read as sheet music. |
| For the songs you're writing and the songs you're learning. | Honest, long. Fits a store listing's first sentence. |
| Where musicians work on songs by ear. | Names the difference from a DAW and from a chart app. |

Noun check: notebook carries both sides; workbook reads as school exercises;
sketchbook undersells rehearsal; songbook is already a feature name.

Store listing shape under consideration: the line, then three moments (a
melody at 2 a.m., the six songs you need by Friday, last night's rehearsal
with the bridge marked), then the tools as proof.

### Open fork: which side is the wedge

The doc above proposed the voice-memo songwriter. The conversation suggested
the band side may be the stronger commercial wedge, with writing as the free
on-ramp. Undecided. This decides what the store listing leads with.

### Not yet researched

The rehearsal-side rivals: BandHelper, OnSong, Setlist Helper, Planning
Center (worship teams, a large weekly-rehearsal market). The founder's claim
that they stop at "here are the songs, in a downloadable format" has not been
tested.

### Premium feel gap

Founder's observation: next to the rivals the app does not feel premium.
Not a call for sleek motion or endless interaction. Certain controls look
like basic components and feel thrown together: pins, the looper, some
buttons, and more beneath. Reads like a simple JS app by an indie developer
rather than a well-built quiet place to finish work. Past facelifts helped
but were not enough. The founder does not want to drive this alone.

The bar, in the founder's words: minimalist and intuitive, calm and beautiful,
smooth like Apple Voice Memos, haptics that match the interaction, quick small
interaction animations. Not sleek motion for its own sake.

Reference apps and what each contributes:

| App | Contributes |
|---|---|
| Apple Voice Memos | The reel feel. Waveform as interface, scrub inertia, no chrome, no layout shift. |
| Flighty | Information hierarchy. Dense data, one reading order, tabular numerals, colour once per screen. |
| Things 3 | Interaction choreography. Every gesture has a short specific animation and a matching haptic. Nothing bounces. |
| Halide | Physical controls. Dials with haptic detents at each stop. Model for looper edges, nudges, the mark slider, the tempo wheel. |

The bar as checkable standards:

| Founder's words | Per control |
|---|---|
| Smooth | No layout shift on state change. Scrub and drag track the finger in the same frame. Lists never jump. |
| Haptics match | A tick at every discrete boundary: segmented thumb landing, each nudge step, loop edge snapping to a section, pin drop. Nothing on continuous motion. |
| Quick small animations | Under 200 ms. Fade or a slide of 6 px or less. Thumbs slide, counts tick, nothing bounces or pops. |
| Minimalist, intuitive | One primary action per screen. Icons only where unambiguous. Controls look like what they do. |
| Calm, beautiful | Hairlines and tonal layers instead of borders. Shared baselines. Scarce colour. |

`motion.ts` and `haptics.ts` already state most of this. The gap is that it
is not applied at the detent level and the controls that would carry it are
still generic.

Proposed method, not started:

- Audit by component, not by screen. One control fixed lifts every screen
  that uses it.
- Walk every screen in the simulator with screenshots and score each control
  against a craft checklist: shared control heights and radii, visible
  pressed and disabled states, baseline alignment, tabular numerals,
  hairlines and tonal layering instead of borders, one icon set at one
  stroke weight and optical size, custom slider thumb and track, custom
  switch, no RN default widgets visible (Slider, Switch, ActivityIndicator,
  Alert, default text selection), state changes that fade rather than swap,
  hit areas that match visual bounds, copy that fits without truncation.
- Produce a ranked list of the ten worst offenders with before screenshots,
  then fix them in order, each verified against the same screenshot.
