# Feature marketing language — how each feature helps, and how they connect

Companion to `final-sweep-2026-10-22/feature-inventory.md` (what exists) and
`store-listing.md` (what we have pasted into the stores so far). This document is the
language layer: for every feature, what it does *for* the person, which part of the
bigger story it serves, and ready-to-use copy. Use it for the store description, the
landing page, screenshots, the welcome flow, the paywall rows and support replies.

Voice rules carry over from the product: calm, concrete, no exclamation marks, no
"simply", no "just". Say what happens. Marketing outside the app can be a little warmer
than copy inside it, but it still sounds like the same person.

Vocabulary: idea (anything in progress) · clip (a recorded fragment) · sketch (a song in
progress) · take (a recording inside a sketch) · song (finished) · collection · workspace ·
layer · mark (section or pin) · spark · parcel · Send.

---

## 1. The macro story

Every feature belongs to one of four moves. Say the four moves everywhere; they are how
a stranger understands that the pieces are one thing and not a toolbox.

| Move | The promise | What the person feels |
|---|---|---|
| **Catch** | The idea gets recorded before it is gone, with enough context to mean something later. | "I didn't lose it." |
| **Keep** | Every idea has a home, a history, and a way back. Nothing is lost, nothing leaves the phone. | "I can find it, and it's safe." |
| **Grow** | A clip becomes a sketch; a sketch gathers takes, words, chords, structure, layers; practice turns it into a song. | "It's getting somewhere." |
| **Share** | The finished thing, or the half-finished thing, travels to a bandmate or a stage without accounts, uploads, or sign-ups. | "Other people can hear it, on my terms." |

The sentence that holds the four together: **"Where voice memos become finished songs."**
Second line, when there is room: **"Catch it in a tap. Keep every version. Grow it into a
song. Share it with a link. Everything stays on your phone."**

The through-line to repeat: *one idea moves through one place.* A hum in the car is a
clip; the clip becomes a sketch; the sketch collects takes and words; the song goes on a
setlist; the setlist goes to the band; what comes back lands in Received. No exporting
between apps, no account in the middle, and the recording you made in the car is still
there, untouched, at the bottom of the thread.

### How things connect (for the "how it works" page)

```
voice memos / a hum in the car / a riff at 2am
        │  Catch: Recorder (count-in, click, pins, lock-screen recording) · Import
        ▼
   Collection of clips  ──── Shelf / Revisit / Activity bring old ones back ────┐
        │  Keep: workspaces, collections, tags, search, 14-day trash, backups    │
        ▼                                                                        │
      Sketch  (takes with versions · lyrics · chart · notes) ◄───────────────────┘
        │  Grow: practice loop, Step up, speed & pitch, layers, editor
        ▼
       Song   → playlist · chart book · setlist (packed with takes, charts, notes)
        │  Share: SongNook Send (7-day link) · export a file · PDF
        ▼
   Bandmate opens the link → Received parcel → keeps it as their own workspace
        → practices, records a layer, sends it back → lands in your Received
```

## 2. Per feature: benefit, connection, copy

Format for each entry:
- **Does** — what it is, in a line.
- **So you can** — the benefit, in the person's terms.
- **Connects** — which move it serves and what it hands to the next feature.
- **Copy** — a headline and one or two sentences you can paste.

### 2.1 Catch

**Recorder (one tap, from anywhere)**
- Does: a record button on every collection and every sketch; the clip lands where you were.
- So you can: record before the idea leaves, without deciding where it goes first.
- Connects: the start of everything. A clip recorded here is already in a collection, already searchable, already backed up by your next backup.
- Copy: **Catch it in a tap.** Record from wherever you are in your library. The clip lands there, named and dated, ready to grow.

**Count-in and the click that is never recorded**
- Does: one to four bars of count-in; a metronome you hear but the recording doesn't.
- So you can: play in time from the first beat and still get a clean take.
- Connects: the take remembers its tempo and meter (the recording grid), which is what gives the player bars and beats, lets the editor snap to the bar, and lets a layer land on the downbeat.
- Copy: **Count yourself in. Keep the click out.** The metronome plays in your ear and never on the tape. The take remembers its tempo, so bars and beats follow you into the player.

**Pins while the tape rolls**
- Does: tap once during a take to drop a marker.
- So you can: mark "that bit" without stopping.
- Connects: pins become marks in the player, places to loop, and part of the parcel when you share.
- Copy: **Mark the moment without stopping.** One tap drops a pin. Later, the pin is where you loop, trim, or start your bandmate.

**Lyrics while you record**
- Does: the sketch's words scroll beside the tape, at your speed.
- So you can: sing the take without juggling a notes app.
- Connects: the take is stamped with the version of the words it was sung to.
- Copy: **Your words, on the tape.** Lyrics scroll while you record, and every take remembers which words it was sung to.

**Recording with the screen locked**
- Does: the take keeps going when the phone locks or you switch apps; lock-screen controls.
- So you can: put the phone down and play.
- Connects: Keep — nothing is cut short; crash recovery keeps a take even if the app dies.
- Copy: **Put the phone down. It keeps recording.** Lock the screen, take the call later. The take is still rolling, and it is kept even if the app is.

**Import: voice memos, files, videos**
- Does: bring in audio from Voice Memos, Files, Drive, or the sound of a video; duplicates are caught.
- So you can: start with the hundreds of "New Recording 47" you already have.
- Connects: Keep — imported clips get the same home, history and search as recorded ones. This is the first-run step.
- Copy: **Bring the ones you already have.** Import your voice memos in a minute. Every "New Recording 47" gets a name, a home, and a way back.

**Bluetooth calibration**
- Does: measures your headphones' delay so layers and the click line up.
- So you can: record over a guide in wireless headphones without the result being late.
- Connects: Grow — layers depend on it.
- Copy: **Wireless headphones, in time.** A thirty-second calibration measures your headset's delay so every layer lands where you played it.

### 2.2 Keep

**Workspaces and collections**
- Does: a workspace per project or band; collections inside it; one level of sub-collections.
- So you can: keep the solo record, the band, and the kids' songs apart without three apps.
- Connects: everything is addressed by where it lives; Send and export work per workspace or collection.
- Copy: **Every idea, one home.** Workspaces for the band, the solo record, the stuff you don't show anyone. Collections inside. Nothing mixed.

**Search by what's inside**
- Does: finds sketches, clips, pages by title, lyrics, chords, notes.
- So you can: find the song by the one line you remember.
- Connects: the retrieval half of Keep; results open the thing in its collection.
- Copy: **Find it by the line you remember.** Search looks inside lyrics, notes and chords, not only titles.

**Shelf**
- Does: set an idea aside for seven days; it asks for a decision at the end.
- So you can: give an idea a deadline instead of a graveyard.
- Connects: Keep → Grow. A shelved idea is a pointer; it never leaves its collection.
- Copy: **Seven days to decide.** Put an idea on the Shelf. It comes back to ask: keep going, or let it go.

**Revisit**
- Does: each day surfaces unfinished work, raw captures, deep cuts, and "this time last year".
- So you can: meet your own old ideas as if someone else sent them.
- Connects: the feature that turns a pile of memos into a practice. Feeds the Shelf and the sketch.
- Copy: **Your old ideas, back on your desk.** Every day Revisit brings up something unfinished, something raw, something from a year ago this week.

**Activity**
- Does: a year of your work as a heatmap; tap a day to see what you made.
- So you can: see that you are actually writing, and find what you made in March.
- Connects: Keep, by time rather than by place.
- Copy: **A year of writing, at a glance.** Tap any day to hear what you made.

**Tags, stages, bookmarks, hide**
- Does: mark clips as riff/verse/chorus; sketches as early/rough/close/song; bookmark; hide the rest.
- So you can: see the five that matter in a collection of two hundred.
- Connects: filters in every list; stage is what makes "song" a state, not a folder.
- Copy: **Riff, verse, chorus. Early, rough, close, song.** Mark what a clip is and how far a sketch has come. Filter to the ones that matter.

**14-day trash and launch-time recovery**
- Does: anything dropped waits fourteen days; anything the library still points at is pulled back on launch.
- So you can: delete without fear.
- Connects: the floor under Keep. Say it once, plainly, on the privacy card.
- Copy: **Deleting is reversible for two weeks.** Takes, layers and clips you remove wait fourteen days in the trash. Nothing disappears by accident.

**Backups you control**
- Does: a verified backup file to Files, iCloud Drive, Drive or a folder; restore by merge or replace; integrity check.
- So you can: move phones, or sleep.
- Connects: the reason "no cloud" is a promise, not a limitation.
- Copy: **Your library, in a file you own.** Back up to Files or Drive whenever you like. Restore on a new phone and every take, version and pin is where you left it.

**No account, nothing uploaded**
- Does: no sign-in; the only network calls are the word tools, book pages, and a link you make on purpose.
- So you can: write songs nobody has heard without wondering where they went.
- Connects: the trust story that makes Share credible ("a link you make on purpose").
- Copy: **Yours, and only yours.** No account, no cloud, no sign-up. Nothing leaves your phone until you send it. No ads. No AI training.

### 2.3 Grow

**Sketch: Takes · Lyrics · Chart · Notes**
- Does: one page per song-in-progress with four tabs.
- So you can: keep the audio, the words, the chords and the thinking in one place.
- Connects: the centre of the app; every Catch feature feeds it, every Share feature packs it.
- Copy: **One page per song.** Takes, lyrics, chart and notes side by side, so the whole song is in one place, not four apps.

**Takes with versions and the Evolution view**
- Does: a new take of the same part stacks as v2, v3 under the first; the Evolution view shows the tree; a primary take marks the current best.
- So you can: try again without losing the first try, and see how the song got here.
- Connects: Keep inside Grow. The original clip is still at the bottom of the thread. This is the "version stacking" people love in review tools, plus the branches.
- Copy: **Every take, in order, nothing overwritten.** Record a new version and the old one stays underneath. Fold a thread open to hear how the song got here.

**Lyric versions stamped per take**
- Does: lyrics have versions; each take knows which version it was sung to; "words edited since this take".
- So you can: change a line without losing the one you sang on the demo.
- Connects: ties Words to Takes; the stage reader and setlists carry the right version.
- Copy: **The words remember the take.** Rewrite a verse and the demo still shows the words you sang. Every version is kept.

**Chords over lyrics and the chart**
- Does: tap a word to place a chord; a chart of sections and bars; "from lyrics" builds one.
- So you can: hand someone the song, not a recording of you explaining it.
- Connects: transpose and capo in the stage reader, PDF export, chart books, setlists.
- Copy: **Chords where the words are.** Tap a word, place a chord. The chart builds itself from the lyrics, and transposes for whoever is playing it.

**Player: marks, sections, pins, bars and beats**
- Does: sections (verse, chorus, bridge) and pins on the reel; bars and beats from the take's own tempo.
- So you can: navigate a song by its parts, not by timestamps.
- Connects: sections are what loops, charts and setlists refer to.
- Copy: **A song has parts. The reel shows them.** Verse, chorus, bridge on the tape, with bars and beats from the tempo you recorded at.

**Practice loop and Step up**
- Does: loop a section; Step up raises the speed a little each pass, on a plan you save.
- So you can: learn the hard bar without changing the recording.
- Connects: Grow → Song. The loop uses the sections you marked.
- Copy: **Loop the hard part. Climb back to speed.** Pick a section, start slow, and Step up a notch every pass until it's yours.

**Speed and pitch, without touching the recording**
- Does: play slower or faster, higher or lower; the file is never changed unless you bake it in the editor.
- So you can: hear the song in a key you can sing, or hear the riff slowly.
- Connects: the stage reader's "keep the shapes with a capo" uses the same math.
- Copy: **Slow it down. Move the key.** Change speed or pitch while you listen. The recording stays as you made it.

**Layers (overdubs)**
- Does: record a harmony or a second part over a take; up to eight layers, one reel, one playhead; solo, mute, nudge; merge or combine.
- So you can: hear the arrangement before there is a band.
- Connects: Grow → Share: a layered take travels in a parcel; a bandmate can add their own.
- Copy: **Stack a harmony over your take.** Record a layer in headphones and hear it on the same reel. Solo it, nudge it, keep it or fold it in. The original take stays untouched underneath.

**Editor: keep, remove, speed & pitch bake**
- Does: keep or cut parts, split a long take into clips, bake a speed or pitch change; always a new clip.
- So you can: cut the good minute out of the ten-minute jam.
- Connects: Keep — the source stays unless you say otherwise; new clips join the sketch as takes.
- Copy: **Keep the good minute.** Mark what to keep, cut the rest. The result is a new clip; the long take is still there.

**Notes, clip notes, take notes**
- Does: free text on the sketch, on a take, on a clip.
- So you can: write "capo 2, try the bridge in minor" where you will see it.
- Connects: notes travel with setlists and parcels.
- Copy: **The note goes where the song is.** Write it on the sketch, on the take, on the clip. It comes along when you share.

**Lyric Pad**
- Does: pages for lines that don't have a song yet; pin, search; a page becomes a lyrics version with one action.
- So you can: keep the line without deciding what it belongs to.
- Connects: Catch for words; feeds sketches and Cut-Up.
- Copy: **A page for the line that came first.** Keep words without a song yet. When the song arrives, the page becomes its lyrics.

**Lyric Spark: Word Ladder, Cut-Up, Borrowed Words**
- Does: three writing games (after Jeff Tweedy's method): pair odd words into lines, cut a lyric up and rebuild it, pull words from a real public-domain book.
- So you can: get unstuck when the page is blank.
- Connects: results send to the Lyric Pad, then to a sketch.
- Copy: **When the page is blank.** Three ways to shake a line loose: pair words that don't belong together, cut your own lyric up and rebuild it, or borrow words from a real book.

**Word Finder**
- Does: rhymes, near rhymes, meanings, imagery; theme words to steer it; definitions; Hebrew too.
- So you can: finish the line without leaving the line.
- Connects: lives inside the lyrics editor and the sparks.
- Copy: **The word that won't come.** Rhymes, near-rhymes, kinds-of and parts-of, steered by the words already in the song.

**Tuner and metronome**
- Does: a chromatic tuner; a metronome with meters, feels, subdivisions, cues.
- So you can: not open another app.
- Connects: the metronome is the same engine as the recorder's click.
- Copy: **The two tools you'd otherwise switch apps for.** A tuner and a metronome that already know your song's tempo.

### 2.4 Share

**SongNook Send (a link, seven days, no account)**
- Does: pack a clip, a sketch, a collection, a setlist or a whole workspace into one link; the other person opens it in the app or in a browser; it expires in seven days.
- So you can: send the demo to the band tonight without a service, a login, or a file-size dance.
- Connects: the only feature that reaches a non-user; the recipient page is where new people meet the app.
- Copy: **Send it with a link. No accounts, either side.** Pack a take, a sketch or a whole setlist into one link. They open it in the app or a browser. It expires in a week.

**Received (parcels, kept apart from your work)**
- Does: what others send lands in Received; look first, then keep it as your own workspace, or into a collection.
- So you can: hear your bandmate's version without it getting mixed into yours.
- Connects: closes the loop: a parcel kept becomes a sketch you can practise, layer, and send back.
- Copy: **What they sent, kept apart until you say so.** Parcels land in Received. Look first. Keep it, and it becomes yours to practise, layer and send back.

**Working with a bandmate (the honest claim today)**
- Does: send a sketch; they keep it; they record a layer or a new take; they send it back; it lands in your Received with their name on it.
- So you can: write together across two phones without a shared account.
- Connects: Share → Catch on the other phone. (Comments on the tape and a web listen page are on the backlog; do not claim them yet.)
- Copy: **Write it together, one parcel at a time.** Send the sketch. Your bandmate keeps it, adds a part, sends it back. It arrives with their name on it, next to your original.

**Setlists, chart books, playlists**
- Does: a setlist packs each song with the take, its sections and pins, the chart and the notes; a chart book is the charts; a playlist is the audio.
- So you can: walk on stage with the whole set on one page, and hand the band the same thing.
- Connects: the Share form of Grow; one file for the whole gig.
- Copy: **The whole set, one page.** Each song with its take, chart and notes. Send the set to the band as one link.

**Stage reader (key, capo, reference audio)**
- Does: lyrics or chart, large; transpose; capo with "keep the shapes"; the take plays in the new key; a click at the song's tempo.
- So you can: play the song in the singer's key tonight.
- Connects: uses the chart and the lyric version the setlist chose.
- Copy: **Tonight's key, tonight's capo.** Read the chart on stage, move the key, keep the shapes, and hear the take in the new key.

**Export: SongNook archive, ZIP, PDF, text**
- Does: a whole workspace or library as an archive; a plain ZIP with .txt lyrics and notes; charts as PDF or ChordPro-style text.
- So you can: leave with everything, or print the chart.
- Connects: the exit door that makes "local-first" trustworthy.
- Copy: **Leave with everything.** Export a workspace or your whole library as a file. Charts as PDF. Lyrics and notes as text.

**Hebrew, properly**
- Does: full Hebrew UI, right-to-left done per string, Hebrew rhymes and meanings, Hebrew book pages for Borrowed Words.
- So you can: write in Hebrew in an app that was built for it, not translated afterwards.
- Connects: a market with almost no competition.
- Copy: **בעברית, כמו שצריך.** A songwriting app that reads right-to-left where it should and keeps the tape left-to-right where it must.

## 3. Messages by person

Use these to pick which features lead on a given surface.

- **The voice-memo hoarder** ("I have 400 memos and no songs"): Import → Collection → Revisit → Shelf → Sketch. Headline: *Where voice memos become finished songs.*
- **The bandmate** ("I need to get this demo to three people tonight"): Send → Received → Layers → Send back. Headline: *Send it with a link. No accounts, either side.*
- **The stage musician** ("different key every gig"): Setlists → Stage reader → Transpose/capo → Reference audio. Headline: *Tonight's key, tonight's capo.*
- **The lyricist** ("the music is fine, the words aren't"): Lyric Pad → Word Finder → Lyric Spark → Lyric versions. Headline: *When the page is blank.*
- **The private writer** ("I don't want this anywhere"): No account → Backups you own → 14-day trash. Headline: *Yours, and only yours.*
- **The Hebrew songwriter**: Hebrew UI → Hebrew word tools → Ben-Yehuda pages. Headline: *בעברית, כמו שצריך.*
