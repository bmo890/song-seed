# Raise Pro's price to double SongNook's revenue

SongNook's planned price ($11.99 a year, $1.99 a month, $29.99 lifetime) is about a third of what similar apps charge, and the evidence says the low price will not bring in more paying users to make up for it. Across 115,000+ subscription apps, low-priced apps turn fewer downloads into payers (1.4%) than mid-priced (2.0%) or high-priced ones (2.8%), and they earn about one sixth as much per payer in the first year ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)). The closest rivals charge $25–40 a year: Demo charges $39.99 and the musician-memo app "Music Memos" charges $24.99. A ladder of **Free / Pro $3.99 a month / $24.99 a year / $79.99 lifetime** roughly doubles expected revenue, even if not one extra person pays. At a typical 2% of downloads paying, that is about **$44k in year one from 100,000 downloads, against $22k on the $1 plan**. At 1 million downloads it is about $440k against $220k. The market is real but the paid slice is small. The direct songwriting-sketchbook leader, Demo, has only about 6,000 US ratings, while free tools like Apple Voice Memos sit on over a million. So SongNook should expect a modest, steady business, not a giant one. Later growth comes most reliably from a band or sync tier with seats and shared setlists. All revenue figures here are our own estimates, built from medians. Treat them as a likely range, not a promise.

**How sure are we?** Each number below carries a tag. **Solid** means an official figure or a large benchmark dataset. **Rough** means a proxy, such as store rating counts or install brackets. **Guess** means third-party revenue estimates or our own modeling. Third-party revenue trackers can be badly wrong: one developer saw Sensor Tower report $40k a month against a real $90k ([Mirava](https://www.mirava.io/blog/app-revenue-growth-estimator)), and estimates for Yousician differ about 30 times between trackers ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/yousician/__9_js14rTh79nrKlExWy_o-y8h6uzWU-JoYbthACpUYg); [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/yousician)).

## Direct rivals are small; the giants sell something else

SongNook competes in four rings. The inner ring is songwriting and idea-capture apps. They are small. **Demo: Songwriting Studio**, the closest match, has about 6,000 US ratings. It charges $5.99 a month, $39.99 a year, or $99.99 lifetime ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1563264178)). Lyric tools are bigger: **Rhymer's Block** has about 34,500 US ratings ([iTunes API](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=rhymers+block&entity=software&country=us)), and the Android "Lyrics Notepad" has 1M+ installs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kmcilvai.perfectpoet)). A crop of new musician-memo apps appeared after Apple retired its own Music Memos app in March 2021 ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/03/02/apple-retires-music-memos/)). They are tiny so far, but they price high: Dubnote, Song Cage, and Nomad's "Music Memos". Several well-loved recorders were abandoned by their makers, among them iZotope's Spire (16,000 US ratings, last updated 2021) and Reason's Take ([iTunes API](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=song+ideas+recorder&entity=software&country=us)). That makes trust and longevity a real selling point in this niche.

The outer rings are much bigger, but they sell different things. **BandLab** has 100M+ users, but it is a social studio, not a private sketchbook ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/business/tech/bandlab-100-million-users-report-1235637853/)). **Moises** reports 70M users for stems and practice ([Moises](https://moises.ai/newsroom/company-milestones/2025-year-in-review/)). **Ultimate Guitar** reports about 30M monthly users ([Muse Group](https://www.mu.se/ultimate-guitar)). Tuners and recorders are mass-market and cheap: GuitarTuna has 100M+ Play installs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ovelin.guitartuna)), and Easy Voice Recorder has 50M+ installs with a $4.99 one-time Pro ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.coffeebeanventures.easyvoicerecorder)). SongNook's real rival is free: Apple Voice Memos, with **1.1M US ratings** ([iTunes API](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=voice+memos&entity=software&country=us)).

Hard revenue numbers for these apps barely exist. Most are private, and the tracker estimates are weak. Sensor Tower once put Moises at about $1M a month from US iPhones alone, but that figure was months old ([Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/1515796612?country=US), Guess). A smaller group of committed musicians pays $25–60 a year for tools they rely on.

## Musicians pay $25–40 a year, mostly on yearly plans

The price band is clear. Across all subscription apps, the typical yearly price is $29.99–39.99, and the median rose to **$34.80** in 2025 ([SaaStr on RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth), Solid). The musician-memo and songwriting apps sit right in that band, at $3.99–7.99 a month and $24.99–39.99 a year. Lifetime options are common in this niche, at $49.99–99.99. Simple recorders and practice tools sell for $5–15 once. SongNook's $11.99 a year is below the bottom of the common band. Its $1.99 a month is four to five times below the usual monthly price.

As for how often people pay: across all apps, about 42% of subscriptions sold are monthly and 34% yearly ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), Solid). Yearly plans keep people far better. **44% of yearly payers are still paying after one year, against 17% of monthly payers**, and barely 10% of monthly payers reach a second year ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025), Solid). Nearly 30% of yearly subscribers turn off auto-renew in the first month ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch), Solid). Most free apps convert about **2% of downloads to paid** within 35 days, and the top quarter manage about 4.5% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), Solid). No benchmark covers music apps alone. The closest categories, Photo & Video and Entertainment, sit below the median ([Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-report/), Rough). iPhone users matter most: iOS brings about 85% of subscription revenue, and iOS users pay about three times as often as Android users ([Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-report/), Solid).

## A low price lowers value per payer without adding payers

This is the central finding. In RevenueCat's data, **low-priced apps convert 1.4% of downloads, mid-priced 2.0%, and high-priced 2.8%** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), Solid). Cheaper apps also get fewer people to start a trial: 4.4% against 8.9% ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)). Meanwhile, first-year value per payer on yearly plans is **$10.69 for low-priced apps against $62.19 for high-priced ones** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), Solid). One analysis puts this down to buyer intent: a cheap price draws a wider but less committed crowd ([Airbridge](https://airbridge.io/en/blog/app-pricing-test), opinion).

The low price does help in two ways. Cheap yearly plans keep more people (53.7% against 48.3% after one year), and people who start a trial on a cheap app convert more often ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025), Solid). But those gains are small next to a 2–3 times lower price. One caution: this data shows patterns, not cause and effect. Better apps may simply charge more. That is why the scenarios below give both plans the **same** conversion rate. We do not assume a higher price brings more payers. We only assume it does not lose many.

Three other choices change the result. **Lifetime price.** Lifetime is usually 5–12 times the yearly price, and examples range from 2.1x to 11.5x ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions.md), Solid). SongNook's $29.99 is 2.5x, near the very bottom. If the price later rises, that creates a large, permanent group of Pro users who pay nothing more. **The trial.** Store trials that take payment details convert 42–46% of trial users when they run 17–32 days. A no-card trial is different, because nobody is billed automatically. With no mobile benchmark for no-card trials, it is safest to treat SongNook's plan like freemium, at about 2% of downloads. Software companies that moved from plain freemium to this kind of full-access trial saw 10–40% more conversions ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/en-us/blog/reverse-trial.md); [Failory](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/no-freemium), SaaS data, Rough). A 30-day window suits SongNook: people store real work in it, and losing Pro tools after that hurts. **Fees and costs.** Small developers pay 15% to Apple or Google. Apple drops to 10% on subscriptions after a subscriber's first year ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622), Solid). RevenueCat is free up to $2,500 a month in revenue, then takes 1% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing/)). Server costs stay tiny because files live on the phone. Our estimate for the 7-day transfer at 1M users is about $20–25 a month ([Cloudflare R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), Guess).

## What 10k, 100k and 1M downloads would earn

The market of possible users is large. MIDiA counted about **75.9 million music creators** worldwide before AI tools inflated the count ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/music-creator-economy-report-key-drivers/), Solid but dated). By that count, 10,000 downloads is about 0.01% of musicians (a small slice), 100,000 is 0.1% (medium), and 1 million is about 1% (large). The large slice is rare. Only **4.6% of new apps reach $10k a month in revenue within two years**, and 17.3% reach $1k ([SaaStr on SOSA 2026](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth), Solid). One million downloads would put SongNook beside Android's biggest lyric notepad, and well above Demo on any size measure we have.

The model is simple. Downloads × share who pay × net money per payer in year one. Net money per payer comes from a plan mix, the 15% store fee, and about 10% off for VAT, refunds and tools. No source gives a plan mix for this category, so the mix is our assumption. It sits inside the 65–80% yearly range used in the research notes.

| Founder's plan: $11.99/yr · $1.99/mo · $29.99 lifetime | 70% / 15% / 15% | $10.19 / ~$9.30 (about 5.5 paid months) / $25.49 | **~$11** | Guess; matches RevenueCat's $8–11 low-price median |
| Recommended: $24.99/yr · $3.99/mo · $79.99 lifetime | 75% / 15% / 10% | $21.24 / ~$15.25 (about 4.5 paid months) / $67.99 | **~$22** | Guess |

Year one, at the median 2% of downloads paying, looks like this. "Free users" means people who downloaded and did not pay. Many of them will stop using the app, so this is not a count of daily users.

The range is wide. The low case is 1% paying, roughly what low-priced apps see. The high case is 4%, near the top quarter.

Two checks support these numbers. First, the founder's plan earns about $0.22 per download in year one, and the recommended plan about $0.44. RevenueCat's freemium median is about $0.38 per download at day 60 ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), Solid). One indie portfolio earned about $0.42 per download from 120,000 downloads ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/50-000-revenue-in-one-year-0276d6a7df), Rough). Second, the higher price wins even if it costs payers. **The recommended plan at 1% paying earns the same as the $1 plan at 2%.** So the higher price only loses if it cuts the number of payers in half, and the data shows no sign of that. If downloads hold steady, yearly renewals pile up. By year three, yearly revenue should run about 1.5–1.7 times the year-one figure. That is our estimate (Guess), based on yearly renewals of about 48–54% ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). The plan mix matters too. An Android-heavy audience, or one mostly outside North America, could cut every figure by half or more. iOS users in North America earn about twice what Android users do per install ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth), Solid).

## Bands, sync and export are the proven next tiers

Similar apps grew past one Pro plan in a few repeatable ways. The best fit for SongNook is a **band or group tier**. OnSong sells Essentials ($29.99 a year: 2 devices, 2 GB), Premium ($59.99 a year: 4 devices, 5 GB, stage features), and a Group plan with seats and shared storage ([OnSong pricing](http://onsongapp.com/pricing/), Solid). An older OnSong page reportedly listed $159 a year for 5 users and $299 for 10 (unverified). BandHelper sells one subscription that covers the whole band ([BandHelper](https://www.bandhelper.com/main/SLM_comparison.html), Solid). SongNook already has setlists, songbooks, chord charts and file transfer. Shared band setlists and charts follow naturally.

Second is **sync and backup across devices**, sold as storage and device count rather than as "backup". OnSong uses gigabytes and device count as its tier fences. An encrypted, opt-in sync would fit "private by default". No source shows whether privacy itself earns a higher price. Third is **export of stems, MIDI and files to a computer music app**. BandLab puts this inside its paid tier rather than selling it separately ([BandLab](https://blog.bandlab.com/bandlab-membership/), Solid). For SongNook, "export layers as stems" belongs inside Pro. Later and harder options include **school licences** (Soundtrap charges about $5 per student a year, with a 50-seat minimum ([Soundtrap EDU](https://edu.soundtrap.com/?p=5473), Rough)), which need teacher tools SongNook lacks. Content catalogs and AI processing, as at Ultimate Guitar or Moises, do not fit a quiet sketchbook.

Privacy is a reason to stay, not proven as a reason to buy. Adobe, SoundCloud and WeTransfer each faced loud backlash over AI-training terms between 2024 and 2025, and each backed down within weeks ([MBW on SoundCloud](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-fixes-ai-policy-following-backlash-ceo-eliah-seton-says-ai-should-support-artists-not-replace-them/); [AlternativeTo on WeTransfer](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/wetransfer-updates-terms-after-backlash-over-potential-use-of-user-content-for-ai-training/)). In a 2026 survey of 1,200 US musicians, 41% rejected training AI on others' work without permission ([Muse Group](https://www.mu.se/posts/muse-group-ai-survey), Solid). The WeTransfer episode bears directly on SongNook's 7-day transfer. A plain-English promise of "no training on your work" protects trust there. No public data ranks these add-ons by how many upgrades they drive. Treat the order above as judgment, not measurement.

## What this means for SongNook

**Recommendation: launch at Free / Pro $3.99 a month / $24.99 a year / $79.99 lifetime, and keep the 30-day full-access trial with no card.**

The reasons are short. Your planned price earns about half as much and does not buy you more payers. $24.99 a year sits in the low part of the musician-app band. It is under Demo and Dubnote ($39.99) and OnSong ($29.99), and it matches Nomad's Music Memos. That keeps SongNook the friendly-priced choice without looking cheap. At $3.99, the monthly plan costs about twice the yearly plan over twelve months, which steers people to yearly, the plan that keeps payers longest. A $79.99 lifetime is 3.2 times the yearly price. That sits inside a sensible 3–5x range, and below Demo's $99.99 and Dubnote's $99.99. It also avoids locking in a large group of near-free Pro users. If you want a launch gesture, sell lifetime as an early-bird at $59.99 for a limited time, then raise it. SongSheet Pro has used early-bird pricing this way ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id581094194)).

| | Founder's plan | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | $1.99 | **$3.99** |
| Yearly | $11.99 | **$24.99** |
| Lifetime | $29.99 (2.5x yearly) | **$79.99 (3.2x yearly)** |
| Trial | 30 days, full access, no card | Same |
| Year-1 net per payer (Guess) | ~$11 | ~$22 |
| 10k downloads, 2% pay | ~$2,200 | ~$4,400 |
| 100k downloads, 2% pay | ~$22,000 | ~$44,000 |
| 1M downloads, 2% pay | ~$220,000 | ~$440,000 |
| Payers needed to match the founder's plan | — | Half as many |

Three practical points follow. First, keep capture free and generous. Voice Memos is free, and the tuner and metronome are free everywhere, so these features will not sell Pro. Charge for the finishing work: layers, sketches, charts with setlists, and export. Second, the trial cannot be judged for 30 days, so watch it closely. If conversion stays under about 1% after two or three months, test adding a 7-day store trial on the yearly plan at the paywall. Store trials that take payment details convert far better, at the cost of a heavier screen. Third, price is the cheapest lever you have, and it is easier to start higher than to raise prices later. Lifetime buyers and early subscribers keep their old price for good.

The main uncertainty is conversion. No one publishes music-app benchmarks, so 2% is borrowed from all freemium apps. Android-heavy or non-US downloads would pull it down. Treat every revenue number as plus or minus half. The price comparison is the solid part: whatever the true conversion, the recommended ladder earns about twice as much per payer.

## Conclusion

The question is not whether musicians will pay $1. It is whether $1 is worth anything to them, and the evidence says cheap reads as low commitment on both sides. SongNook's best case is not to win on price against Voice Memos. It is to be the place serious songwriters keep their unfinished work, priced like the tools they already trust ($25–40 a year) and backed by a promise that their work stays theirs. The abandoned recorders in this niche (Spire, Take, Hum) show that musicians fear their tools vanishing. A fair, sustainable price tells them this one will last.

The bigger money comes later and from groups. The largest increases in this category came from adding a second customer type, such as bands, schools or businesses, rather than squeezing more out of individuals. For SongNook the natural next step is a band tier built on what exists: setlists, songbooks and transfer, plus shared charts and opt-in encrypted sync. If OnSong's older group prices still hold, one band could be worth as much as six to twelve single Pro users. That makes the band tier the clearest route beyond what a single Pro plan can earn.
