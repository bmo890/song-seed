# Revenue expansion paths for songwriting / musician apps (2023-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-19. Method note: pages marked [fetched] were read directly; everything else comes from web-search result summaries. Treat those as secondhand until checked on the vendor's page. App Store prices vary by region and change often.

## Which features most drive willingness to pay (expansion paths)

### Takeaway
In this category, the proven ways to grow past a single subscription are: (1) a higher tier that bundles the "working musician on stage" features (backing tracks, multitracks, stage control, more cloud storage and devices), as OnSong does; (2) group/band seats with shared storage (OnSong Group, BandHelper's one subscription for the whole band); (3) content or catalog bundles (lessons/courses, sounds, tabs) layered onto the tool (Ultimate Guitar, Splice, Yousician, BandLab); (4) per-seat education licenses (Soundtrap); (5) B2B licensing of the underlying tech (Moises' Music.AI); (6) one-time purchase plus a small optional annual "Pro" (forScore, Anytune). I found no public data that ranks these by conversion. Cloud storage and device count show up as the dividers between tiers again and again, which suggests they are reliable fences.

- **DAW/companion pricing anchor**: Logic Pro for iPad launched 2023-05-23 at $4.99/mo or $49/yr. — [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2023/05/apple-officially-intros-logic-pro-for-ipad-daw-goes-mobile-and-multi-touch.html); [Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/apple-unveils-final-cut-pro-and-logic-pro-for-ipad/)

### Inferences
- Cloud is sold as storage plus devices plus shared libraries, not as "backup". OnSong prices it in GB and device count, and the Group plan's hook is shared storage. A local-first app like SongNook could sell optional end-to-end-encrypted sync/backup as a fence without giving up "private by default". This is my inference; I found no evidence on whether E2EE sync carries a price premium.
- Band/group seats look like the clearest "team" path for songwriter/performer tools (OnSong, BandHelper). A setlist-plus-charts app maps directly onto it: shared setlists and charts for a band.
- Stems/MIDI export and desktop/DAW hand-off are bundled as Pro perks (BandLab); none of the products I found sell them as standalone add-ons. For SongNook, "export layers as stems / to Logic/GarageBand/Ableton" fits naturally inside the Pro tier.
- Education licenses are proven (Soundtrap at ~$5/seat/yr, 50-seat minimum). They need a collaboration or teacher layer and classroom admin (rostering) that SongNook doesn't have. It's a later-stage path.
- One-time purchase plus a small annual Pro (forScore) is a proven, low-friction model for a privacy-first, no-server app.

### Gaps
- No public conversion or ARPU data showing which add-on (cloud, collaboration, export, courses) drives the most upgrades. Companies don't publish this.
- No current, verified prices for OnSong Group seats, BandHelper tiers or Soundtrap EDU from the vendors' own pages (the pages either hide prices or didn't load).
- Printing/PDF export of chord charts appears as a base feature (OnSong Essentials includes "setlist emailing, exporting, and printing"), not a premium one. I found no evidence it drives upgrades on its own.
- No data on marketplaces (templates, beats) for songwriting-notebook apps specifically. Splice and BandLab Sounds are producer-oriented.

## Case studies: how category apps expanded (dated)

### Takeaway
The big players grew by (a) adding tiers above a single Pro (BandLab Pro/Max, Splice Sounds+/Creator/Creator+, Yousician Premium+/Family), (b) buying content or catalog (Muse Group buying Hal Leonard in 2023, Splice buying Spitfire in 2025), (c) spinning out B2B (Moises Music.AI), and (d) education seats (Soundtrap). Smaller utility apps (forScore, Anytune, OnSong) stay with one-time or low-priced tiers plus band/pro add-ons. Soundtrap shows that a platform owner can drop a creation tool: Spotify sold it back to its founders in June 2023.

### Cited Findings
- **BandLab**: Free / Pro / Max membership. Pro bundles Cakewalk Next & Sonar desktop DAWs, distribution and 7-stem splitting; Max adds pro vocal and mastering tools and $50/month of promotion credits [fetched blog]. Reported prices vary: US App Store $14.99/mo or $149.99/yr per one listing; Black Friday 2025 annual deal worked out to ~$5.80/mo (60% off). The blog cites "100M+ creators". — [BandLab blog](https://blog.bandlab.com/bandlab-membership/); [MusicTech Black Friday](https://musictech.com/news/gear/bandlab-membership-lowest-price-ever-black-friday); [BandLab App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/968585775)
- **Splice**: three tiers ($12.99 / $19.99 / $39.99 per month). Launched the Splice Create mobile app in 2024. Acquired Spitfire Audio 2025-04-28 (~$50M reported); Kakul Srivastava is CEO. — [Splice support](https://support.splice.com/en/articles/8652596-starting-a-new-splice-subscription); [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/news/splice-acquires-spitfire-audio-for-a-reported-50-million/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/splice-buying-spitfire-audio-to-help-creators-find-the-right-sounds/)
- **Moises**: consumer tiers Premium (~$5.99/mo or $39.99/yr US) and Pro (~$29.99/mo) per App Store snapshots (other sources give lower prices; regional variation). B2B arm Music.AI. Raised a $40M Series A (Jan 2025). Registered users went 30M (Apr 2023) → ~50M (Jan 2025, reported). — [App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1515796612); [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/moises-music-ai-services/); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/after-rasing-10-25m-ai-music-making-app-moises-reaches-than-more-30m-registered-users1234/)
- **Yousician**: Premium / Premium+ / Premium+ Family (4 accounts, $209.99/yr). Company-stated recurring revenue grew from $30M to $50M (2021 Series B, $28M raise). 2024 revenue is estimated by aggregators at €53.3M-$57.7M (unverified). — [MBW Series B](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/yousician-raises-28m-in-b-round-new-investors-include-amazon-alexa-fund-and-paul-mccartneys-mpl-ventures); [Yousician family](https://yousician.com/blog/premium-family-memberships); [Lazyweb (aggregator)](https://lazyweb.com/company/yousician)

It says its brands reach "500M+" musicians annually. Francisco Partners exited its minority stake (financed by J.P. Morgan credit facilities). No public revenue. — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/francisco-partners-exits-its-minority-investment-in-muse-group-owner-of-audacity-hal-leonard-and-ultimate-guitar/); [mu.se/about](https://mu.se/about)
- **OnSong**: free (30 songs) → Essentials ($29.99/yr) → Premium ($59.99/yr) → Group (seat-based, shared Drive), plus the browser Console as a web companion. — [OnSong pricing](http://onsongapp.com/pricing/)
- **BandHelper**: Basic / Plus / Pro, with one subscription covering the whole band across platforms. — [BandHelper comparison](https://www.bandhelper.com/main/SLM_comparison.html)
- **forScore**: one-time $24.99 + optional forScore Pro annual ($9.99 or $14.99, conflicting). — [forScore](https://forscore.co/kb/how-to-buy/); [forScore Pro](https://forScore.co/pro)
- **Soundtrap**: EDU per-seat (~$5/student/yr, 50 minimum). ### Inferences
- The closest comparables to SongNook (Hum, Demo, OnSong, forScore, Anytune) price individual tiers at about $30-60/yr. Producer platforms (BandLab, Splice, Moises Pro) run $100-480/yr because they bundle content, distribution or heavy processing.
- Spotify's Soundtrap sale suggests creation tools are hard to monetize inside a larger platform. Independent, focused tools keep going on subscription plus education.

### Gaps
- No public paid-subscriber counts for any of these companies. Revenue figures for Yousician and Splice come only from aggregators.
- Could not confirm the Moises "for teams" plan or the BandLab Membership launch date.

## Is "no AI training / private by default" a selling point?

### Takeaway
Yes, as a trust and retention defence rather than proven acquisition: every 2024-2025 terms-of-service episode (Adobe June 2024, SoundCloud May 2025, WeTransfer July 2025) brought public backlash and cancellation threats, and each company retreated within days to weeks. Surveys show musicians want AI for practice and cleanup but reject generation and non-consensual training. No survey directly measures willingness to pay for "no AI training".

### Cited Findings
- **Adobe (June 2024)**: a forced re-acceptance pop-up with broad content-license language led to cancellation threats. Adobe published clarifications on June 6 and 10 and rewritten terms around June 18-20, saying it does not train generative AI on customer content (except Adobe Stock submissions). Many replies called it "too little, too late". — [CG Channel 2024-06-20](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/06/adobe-updates-its-terms-of-use-following-artist-backlash); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/adobe-backtracks-on-ai-training-policy-update)
- **SoundCloud (May 2025)**: a February 2024 terms change permitting AI use of uploads surfaced in May 2025. Artists urged removing music. On May 9 SoundCloud said it had "never used artist content to train AI models" and promised opt-outs for any future generative training. CEO Eliah Seton then revised the policy ("AI should support artists, not replace them"). — [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/soundcloud-issues-statement-following-terms-of-service-ai-update-backlash); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-fixes-ai-policy-following-backlash-ceo-eliah-seton-says-ai-should-support-artists-not-replace-them/); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/soundcloud-updates-ai-policy-following-backlash-ai-should-support-artists-not-replace-them-news)
- **WeTransfer (July 2025)**: a new clause 6.3 granted a perpetual license including ML training and derivative works. Creatives threatened to cancel. WeTransfer removed the AI/ML wording and narrowed the license to operating and improving the service, saying content was never used for training. This matters for SongNook's 7-day transfer feature. — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/wetransfer-updates-terms-after-backlash-over-potential-use-of-user-content-for-ai-training/); [The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/wetransfer-faces-backlash-over-tos-changes-and-allegations-of-training-ai-on-user-files/); [Slashdot/Guardian](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/25/07/16/165222/wetransfer-backtracks-on-terms-suggesting-user-files-could-train-ai-models-after-backlash)
- **Muse Group survey (published 2026-04-10; 1,200 US musicians using UG/MuseScore/MuseHub/Audacity)** [fetched]: 78% are open to AI tools and 70% already use some. 18% are open to AI generating music from scratch. 41% reject training AI on others' work without permission, and 40% reject copying artist styles without consent. 93% prefer more control even if slower. 34% call unclear copyright a dealbreaker, and 42% want explicit licensing terms. The most accepted uses are cleanup (70%), practice help (68%) and transcription (58%); the least are lyrics (37%) and auto-finishing (32%). Ages 18-24 show the highest opposition (28%). (A search summary described a "strong majority" opposing training without consent; the primary page says 41%.) — [Muse Group](https://www.mu.se/posts/muse-group-ai-survey)
- **GEMA/SACEM, Goldmedia (published 2024-01-30; ~15,000 members surveyed late 2023)**: 71% of creators fear AI will take their income. 35% have used AI in their music work (51% of under-35s). 19% are "AI refusers" and 26% "would rather not". The study estimates 27% of creator revenue at risk by 2028 (€2.7bn cumulative). — [GEMA AI study](https://www.gema.de/AI-study); [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/generative-ai-music-market-to-be-worth-3bn-by-2028-says-report-commissioned-by-sacem-and-gema)
- **Tracklib (2025, 1,734 producers)**: 82% are against full text-prompt song generation, and only 6% use fully generative tools. — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/music-producers-are-rejecting-ai-study-reveals-over-80-of-producers-are-against-ai-generated-songs)
- **University of Alberta (Canadian musicians, ~300 respondents)**: 87% don't want to use AI in their music (small sample). — [CTV News](https://guelph.ctvnews.ca/edmonton/article/canadian-musicians-feel-ai-is-disrupting-their-process-study/)
- **Sound On Sound / Sonarworks (2026, ~1,200 creators)**: more than a third worry AI reliance would compromise creative intent. — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/music-business/ai-music-tech-2026?amp)

### Inferences
- "Private by default, never trained on, on-device" lines up with documented anxieties: training without consent, unclear licensing, loss of control. It is strongest as a trust and switching message aimed at users who left or distrust cloud platforms (SoundCloud, WeTransfer), and as a durable promise written in plain-English terms.
- The resistance concentrates on generation and lyrics. Musicians do accept assistive, non-generative tools (transcription, cleanup, practice). SongNook's "AI-free" stance therefore lands best as "no generation, no training on your work". An absolute anti-technology stance could alienate the 70% who use some AI.
- Younger creators (18-24) are the most opposed (Muse Group), which is useful for targeting.

### Gaps
- No survey measures conversion or willingness to pay for "no AI training" or "on-device" claims in music apps.
- No direct evidence that the backlashes caused measurable cancellations (only threats and anecdotes).

## Typical tier structures and price levels for cloud / collab tiers

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is Free (capped) → Individual tier(s) at ~$30-60/yr for musician utilities → a group, family or band tier at about 3-10x the individual price, with seats and shared storage. Producer and content platforms sit higher ($100-480/yr). The alternative is one-time purchase plus a ~$10-15/yr Pro.

### Cited Findings
- **OnSong**: Free (30 songs) / Essentials $29.99/yr, 2 GB, 2 devices / Premium $59.99/yr, 5 GB, 4 devices / Group with seats and shared Drive. Older Group prices were reportedly $159/yr for 5 users and $299/yr for 10. — [OnSong](http://onsongapp.com/pricing/); [OnSong group (older)](https://onsongapp.com/pricing/group)
- **Yousician**: Premium+ Family (4 accounts) $209.99/yr. — [Yousician family](https://yousician.com/blog/premium-family-memberships)
- **Splice**: $12.99 / $19.99 / $39.99 per month. — [Splice support](https://support.splice.com/en/articles/8652596-starting-a-new-splice-subscription)
- **BandLab**: Free / Pro (~$14.99/mo or $149.99/yr US listing; deep discounts common) / Max. — [BandLab App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/968585775); [BandLab blog](https://blog.bandlab.com/bandlab-membership/)
- **Moises**: Free / Premium ~$39.99/yr / Pro ~$29.99/mo (US App Store snapshot). — [App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1515796612)
- **Demo: Songwriting Studio**: Demo+ $5.99/mo or $39.99/yr. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1563264178)
- **Logic Pro for iPad**: $4.99/mo or $49/yr. — [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2023/05/apple-officially-intros-logic-pro-for-ipad-daw-goes-mobile-and-multi-touch.html)
- **forScore**: $24.99 one-time + Pro $9.99-14.99/yr. — [forScore](https://forscore.co/kb/how-to-buy/)
- **Soundtrap EDU**: ~$5/seat/yr, 50-seat minimum. — [EdTech Books](https://edtechbooks.org/onlinetools/soundtrap)

### Inferences
- A plausible SongNook ladder, based on these comparables: Free → Pro ~$30-50/yr (the core individual tier) → a later "Band" or "Sync" tier ~$100-300/yr for 3-10 seats with shared setlists, charts and E2EE storage. Education seats at ~$5/student/yr would come only if a teacher/classroom layer gets built. This is my inference from the comparables, not a measured optimum.
- Storage in GB and device count are the most common, easily understood fences for a cloud tier (OnSong).

### Gaps
- No verified current per-seat price for any band/group plan (OnSong Group and BandHelper hide or omit it).
- No public data on the share of revenue that group/education tiers bring in for any of these companies.
