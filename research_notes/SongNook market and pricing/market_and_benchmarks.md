# Market size for mobile songwriting/musician apps and subscription conversion benchmarks

Research date: 2026-10-19. Every figure is dated. "Official" = published by the primary source (company, store, research firm's own page). "Secondary" = repeated by press or an aggregator. "Estimate" = market-research forecast or my own inference.

## 1. How many people play an instrument, write songs or make music as a hobby?

### Takeaway
The best current global figure is MIDiA Research's 148.7 million "music creators" in 2025, but it includes generative-AI music users. The underlying base of human musicians grew only 33% from 2020 to 2025. The older (2022) count of 75.9M creators is closer to "people who actually make music". National instrument-playing rates range from about 10–12% (UK government survey, playing in the past year) to about 43–52% (broad "make music" or "anyone in the household plays" wording). Wording drives the number, so any funnel must use a narrow definition: people who write songs, not people who own a guitar.

### Cited Findings
**Global (MIDiA Research; official firm figures, mostly paywalled)**
- 148.7 million global music creators in 2025, including gen-AI music users. Music creators (incl. AI) grew 167% over 2020–2025 while musicians grew 33%. Gen-AI music MAUs nearly tripled 2023–2025 and added about 9× as many users as the musician base. Gen AI is forecast to reach 38% of music software, sounds and services revenue by 2033 (MIDiA blog, 9 Jul 2026) — [MIDiA: The future of the music creator economy](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/the-future-of-the-music-creator-economy)
- 75.9 million global music creators at end-2022 (+12% YoY), with a forecast of 198.2 million by 2030. The two figures may use different definitions from the 2025 number (secondary coverage of the MIDiA report) — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/music-creator-economy-report-key-drivers/); [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/nearly-200-million-music-creators-predicted-by-2030/). MusicTech articles disagree on whether 75.9M is a 2022 or 2023 figure — [MusicTech (2024 article)](https://musictech.com/news/industry/more-music-released-in-a-day-in-2024-than-entirety-of-1989)
- 6.4 million self-releasing "artists direct" in 2022 (+16.8% vs 2021). Of these, 38% aspire to a full-time music career and 36% are "passionate about music" but expect no career (MIDiA 2023 creator survey; blog dated 10 May 2023). Enthusiasts spend 8% more per year on music-making tools than career aspirants — [MIDiA blog](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/why-the-creator-tools-and-services-market-is-bigger-than-you-think-and-it-is-not-ai)
- MIDiA's 2025 music creator survey had 2,109 respondents. The Q2 2024 survey segments creators by aspiration, experience, spend, time and software, but the figures are paywalled — [MIDiA Music Creator Survey 2025](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/midia-music-creator-survey-2025); [MIDiA report: Recalibration](https://www.midiaresearch.com/reports/music-creator-survey-recalibration)

**Platform counts (official company figures, registered users, not active users)**
- BandLab passed 100 million registered users in March 2024, up 40M since January 2023. About 30% of users are in the US. The earlier figure was 60M+ users creating 16M+ new songs a month. The CEO predicts "over one billion music creators by 2030" (an aspiration, not data) — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-making-app-bandlab-surpasses-100-million-users); [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/bandlab-100-million-users/)

**US**
- NAMM/Gallup: 52% of US households have at least one member aged 5+ who currently plays an instrument (vs 37% UK, 36% Australia). The survey year is unclear. An earlier 2003 release said 54% — [NAMM 2003 press release](https://www.namm.org/news/press-releases/gallup-organization-reveals-findings-american-attitudes-toward-making-music); [NAMM page](https://ww1.namm.org/node/284)
- Fender/YouGov 2021: 7% of Americans aged 13–64 (about 16 million) started playing guitar in the previous two years. 62% of these beginners cited the pandemic, nearly three-quarters are aged 13–34, and 53% practise 2 hours a week or less. A 2018 Fender study found women are 50% of beginner and aspiring players — [Loudwire](https://loudwire.com/study-who-are-16-million-americans-learned-play-guitar-last-two-years/); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/pro/news/fender-study-finds-half-of-new-guitarists-are-women-738025/)

**UK**
- ABRSM *Making Music* 2021 (survey mostly fielded in 2020, 1,505 adults): 43% of adults say they are "actively making music". Adults playing instruments fell about 20% since 2014 — [Youth Music summary](https://www.youthmusic.org.uk/resources/making-music-teaching-learning-and-playing-uk); [Music Teacher](https://www.musicteachermagazine.co.uk/articles/abrsm-issues-warning-over-music-teacher-numbers-in-new-report)
- Government *Taking Part* survey (England, 2016/17): 11.7% played an instrument for their own pleasure in the past 12 months, and 3.6% played to an audience. Making Music's comparison puts Scotland at about 15% and England at about 10% — [Making Music](https://www.makingmusic.org.uk/node/9620); [Making Music: UK participation data](https://www.makingmusic.org.uk/node/35488)

### Inferences
- Defensible range of active hobby or semi-pro musicians worldwide: roughly 75–100M people, using MIDiA's pre-AI creator count. The 148.7M figure is inflated by gen-AI prompt users who are not SongNook's audience.
- People who write original songs, as opposed to only playing covers, are a subset. MIDiA's 6.4M self-releasing artists (2022) is a hard floor for "serious songwriters", and the realistic songwriter pool is probably in the tens of millions globally. No source quantifies "writes songs" directly.
- For the US, a narrow "plays regularly as an adult" rate of about 10–15% is the conservative planning number (UK government wording gives ~12%). The 52% household figure counts children and households, not adults.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 NAMM/Gallup US participation survey was found. The cited US figures are old (2003/undated) or guitar-only (Fender 2021).
- No data found for the EU as a bloc or for Israel.
- No source gives the share of musicians who write original songs.
- MIDiA's segment sizes (hobbyist / semi-pro / pro) and its 2024–2025 creator counts are paywalled.
- One site's claim of "28.5M home-studio creators" attributed to MIDiA was unverifiable and is excluded.

## 2. What share of musicians use mobile apps to capture song ideas or practise?

### Takeaway
No reliable independent survey quantifies this. The evidence is anecdotal or comes from vendors: Apple says many musicians use Voice Memos, and one app maker claims "63% say audio note apps are essential".

### Cited Findings
- Dubnote, an app vendor, claims 63% of musicians say audio note apps are essential but dislike the existing ones. No sample or method is given, and the claim is a launch promotion — [Mixdown Magazine](https://mixdownmag.com.au/?p=73931)
- Apple (Music Memos launch coverage) said a "huge percentage" of musicians it spoke with used Voice Memos to capture ideas. This is unquantified and comes from marketing — [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/articles/23784-recording-guitarist-a-music-memo-demo)
- Songwriters interviewed by Songwriting Magazine describe phone voice memos as their main capture tool (qualitative only) — [Songwriting Magazine](https://www.songwritingmagazine.co.uk/tips/songwriting-tips-five-ways-to-capture-song-ideas)
- Pew's 2004 musician survey (n=2,793, non-random) covered internet use, not mobile capture, and is outdated — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2004/12/05/part-3-the-musicians-survey/)
- BandLab's scale (100M registered users in 2024, 16M+ songs a month at the 60M-user mark) is the strongest proof that mobile music creation is mainstream — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-making-app-bandlab-surpasses-100-million-users)

### Inferences
- Phone voice memos are close to universal among smartphone-owning songwriters, which makes the free built-in Voice Memos app SongNook's real competitor. Paid conversion has to come from organisation and finishing, not from capture alone.

### Gaps
- No quantitative 2024–2026 survey was found on mobile-app use for idea capture or practice. MIDiA's creator survey asks about software used, but its results are paywalled.

## 3. Music-creation software/app market size and growth

### Takeaway
Market-research numbers are low-reliability and inconsistent. Technavio's music production software market adds only about $0.4B over five years at roughly 7.7–7.9% CAGR. MIDiA tracks creator tools as part of a larger creator economy but published no absolute dollar figure in its free material. Treat all of these as directional only.

### Cited Findings
- Technavio (Mar 2024): the music production software market grows by about $415.45M over 2023–2028 at a 7.86% CAGR. A later edition projects +$432.8M over 2024–2029 at 7.7% CAGR. The 2020–2024 edition said +$368M at about 9% CAGR (all paywalled estimates) — [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/music-production-software-market-industry-size-analysis?amp); [Business Wire 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210204005727/en/Music-Production-Software-Market-to-Accelerate-at-a-CAGR-of-Almost-9-during-2020-2024-Technavio)
- MIDiA forecasts gen AI at 38% of music software, sounds and services revenue by 2033, but gave no total in the free post (Jul 2026) — [MIDiA](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/the-future-of-the-music-creator-economy)
- MIDiA (2023) models the streaming-income opportunity for passionate enthusiasts at $88M a year and for career seekers at $96M a year. This is earnings, not tool spend — [MIDiA blog](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/why-the-creator-tools-and-services-market-is-bigger-than-you-think-and-it-is-not-ai)

### Inferences
- The top-down market size is not useful for SongNook. A bottom-up model (reachable musicians × install rate × download-to-paid × annual price) is more defensible.

### Gaps
- No Grand View Research figure was retrieved. Its reports are paywalled and the search did not surface one.
- No dedicated "mobile songwriting app" market size exists in any found source.

## 4. Subscription conversion benchmarks (RevenueCat, Adapty)

### Takeaway
The median freemium subscription app converts about 2% of downloads to paid within 35 days, and the top quartile is about 4.5%. Hard paywalls convert about 10.7% but lose most installs. Trial-to-paid sits around 25–45% depending on trial length and region. Annual plans earn the most per install. About 30% of annual subscribers cancel auto-renew in month one. Refunds run at about 2–5% of transactions, with Adapty's funnel figure at 8.3%. Neither RevenueCat nor Adapty publishes a Music/Audio category.

### Cited Findings
**RevenueCat *State of Subscription Apps 2026* (2025 data; 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue; official)** — [RevenueCat SOSA](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)
- Download-to-paid (D35): overall median 2.0%. Freemium median 2.1% (top quartile >4.5%). Hard paywall median 10.7% (top quartile >20.0%, top 10% at 38.7%).
- Download-to-paid by price tier: low-priced apps 1.4% (TQ >3.7%), mid-priced 2.0% (TQ >4.4%), high-priced 2.8% (TQ >6.1%). Higher prices do not reduce conversion in this data. Download-to-trial is also highest for high-priced apps: 8.9% vs 5.4% mid and 4.4% low.
- Download-to-paid by region: North America median 2.8% (TQ >6.0%; the page also says 2.6% elsewhere, an internal inconsistency), APAC 2.4%, India/SEA 0.7%.
- Download-to-trial (D30) by category: Education 6.5%, Utilities 6.5%, Health & Fitness 6.9%, Media & Entertainment 4.0%, Business 9.1%. By region: North America 7.1%, Western Europe 5.0%.
- Trial-to-paid by trial length: ≤4 days 25.5% (TQ >38.5%), 5–9 days 37.4% (TQ >52.8%), 17–32 days 42.5% (TQ >59.4%). Trials of ≤4 days rose from 42.1% to 46.5% of all trials.
- Trial-to-paid by category: Photo & Video 22.2% (TQ >33.1%), Health & Fitness 37.7%, Travel 43.5%. By region: North America 34.2%, Western Europe 29.7%.
- Revenue per install: overall median $0.23 at D14 and $0.34 at D60. Freemium $0.27/$0.38, hard paywall $2.32/$3.09. By dominant plan (D14/D60): yearly $0.36/$0.46, monthly $0.18/$0.29.
- Realised LTV per payer: yearly plans in high-priced apps $62.19 vs low-priced $10.69. Monthly plans: high-priced $35.89 vs low-priced $6.67. Yearly median for North America is $26.07 (the page also cites $32, an inconsistency).
- Plan mix: about 42% of subscriptions sold are monthly and 34% yearly. Median yearly price rose from $31.60 to $34.80. Medians for monthly ($10) and weekly ($5.99) were flat.
- Median YoY MRR growth was 5.3%, and the top 10% grew >306%.

**RevenueCat *State of Subscription Apps 2025* (2024 data; official launch post)** — [RevenueCat blog](https://revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)
- Nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in the first month.
- Apps with cheap annual plans retain up to 36% of users after one year. High-priced monthly plans retain 6.7%.
- Overall median D60 RPI was $0.31. The top 5% of new apps earn $8,880 after two years, and the bottom 25% earn $19 or less.
- 35% of apps mix subscriptions with consumables or lifetime purchases.
- Secondary write-ups attribute a 1.9% median download-to-paid and an 8.5% top-performer figure to the 2025 edition — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/why-subscription-app-get-installs-no-paid-subscribers). Secondary coverage also says about 10% of monthly subscribers reach a second year — [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)

**Adapty *State of In-App Subscriptions 2026* (2025 data; 16,000+ apps, about $3B revenue, mostly iOS; official)** — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-report/)
- Global funnel: install-to-trial 11.2% (North America 14.5%, elsewhere 7.6–10.2%), trial-to-paid 27.8%. Renewal survival: 1st renewal 59.2%, 2nd 45.1%, 3rd 37.1%, 4th 31.6%, 5th 27.6%. Refund rate 8.3%.
- Search snippets of the same report put Entertainment trial-to-paid at 19.1% (the weakest category) against a 25.6% global average, which conflicts with 27.8%. Adapty's figures differ across its own pages — [Adapty trial conversion blog](https://adapty.io/blog/trial-conversion-rates-for-in-app-subscriptions)
- Median one-year LTV per install: Productivity $1.10, Education $1.06, Photo & Video $0.82, Entertainment $0.59. AI apps $1.44 vs $0.84 for all apps.
- Productivity: about 14% of users remain after one year. Annual plans in Utilities: 22.1% one-year retention.
- Weekly plans were 55.6% of 2025 app revenue, up from 43.3% in 2023. The median annual plan costs about 3× the monthly one.
- iOS earns about 85% of subscription revenue, roughly 5.6× Android. iOS install-to-paid is about 3× Android (3.6× on annual plans).
- European prices index at 1.2× the US (UK, FR, DE, IT, ES) and rose 18% YoY.
- Photo & Video has the highest trial refund rate: 6.4% globally, 14.1% in APAC.

**Refunds (Adapty blog, updated 10 Feb 2026; partly unsourced)** — [Adapty refund benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/refund-rate-metrics-and-benchmarking/)
- Overall 2–5% of paid transactions, citing Adapty and RevenueCat data.
- By plan: weekly 2.6%, monthly 3.0–3.5%, annual 4.2%.
- Productivity 2.5–3.5%. The category source is not stated.
- Google Play has a 48-hour auto-refund window, while Apple reviews requests individually.

### Inferences
- **Planning numbers for a freemium SongNook:** download-to-paid of about 1.5–2% is the base case, and about 4–5% would be top quartile. North America and iOS-heavy installs push this up, while Android and emerging markets pull it down sharply.
- A higher-priced annual plan, rather than a cheap one, is supported by the data: high-priced apps convert as well or better and earn about 6× the LTV per payer. Note that the price tier is defined relative to category.
- Trial length: 5–9 days or longer clearly beats ≤4 days on trial-to-paid.
- Music/Audio has no published category. The nearest proxies are Photo & Video (trial-to-paid 22.2% RC, LTV/install $0.82 Adapty) and Entertainment ($0.59 LTV/install), which suggest music apps sit below the median.
- Rough revenue per 1,000 installs (freemium): about $340–380 at D60 (RevenueCat median RPI), or $590–$1,100 one-year LTV/install (Adapty Entertainment–Productivity). This is an inference that mixes the two datasets.

### Gaps
- No Music/Audio category benchmark was found in RevenueCat or Adapty material.
- No public data was found on reverse trials or no-card trials. The App Store requires no card prompt beyond the Apple ID, so "no-card" mainly applies to web.
- Detailed annual vs monthly renewal rates and the 2024 edition's full funnel are in the full PDFs, which were not retrieved.
- Superwall, Appfigures and Business of Apps data on music-app ARPU was not retrieved within the research budget.

## 5. Store economics and tooling costs

### Takeaway
Small developers keep 85% of net-of-tax revenue on both stores. Apple drops its commission to 10% for subscriptions after a subscriber's first year in the Small Business Program. Google charges 15% on subscriptions, or 10% plus a 5% billing fee in the US/EEA/UK/AU/JP. RevenueCat is free up to $2,500 monthly tracked revenue and charges 1% above that.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Small Business Program: 15% commission on paid apps and in-app purchases for developers with ≤$1M in proceeds in the prior and current calendar year. New developers qualify. Crossing $1M reverts future sales to the standard rate. "For subscriptions after their first year, Apple will offer a further reduced commission of 10%." Proceeds are defined as "sales net of Apple's commission and certain taxes and adjustments" (accessed Oct 2026, official) — [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Google Play: auto-renewing subscriptions are charged 15% regardless of developer revenue in most markets. In the rollout regions (US, EEA, UK, Australia, Japan) the rate is "10% + 5% billing fee" for purchases through Play Billing. Other in-app purchases are 15% on the first $1M a year and 30% above that (accessed Oct 2026, official) — [Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622)
- RevenueCat Pro: free up to $2,500 monthly tracked revenue (MTR), then 1%. MTR is measured on gross revenue, before store fees. Sources conflict on whether the 1% applies to all MTR or only the amount above $2,500. A Q2 2026 pricing-page change moved "Growth Tools" (paywalls) to self-serve Pro without changing the core rate. These are secondary sources, so verify on revenuecat.com/pricing — [PricingSaaS diff log](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/revenuecat/diffs/2026Q2); [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/subscription-billing/revenuecat/); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/revenuecat-alternatives/)

### Inferences
- Store prices in the EU, UK and Israel are VAT-inclusive, and Apple and Google remit VAT before calculating commission. A €4.99 price in a 20% VAT country therefore yields about €4.16 net of VAT, and about €3.54 after a 15% commission. This follows Apple's "net of… certain taxes" definition, but the exact per-country VAT rates were not retrieved. Israel's VAT is 18% from 2025; this comes from training knowledge and is unverified here.
- At small scale (under $2,500 MTR) the stack costs about 15% (store) plus 0% (RevenueCat) plus VAT where applicable. The effective take on a long-retained Apple subscriber falls to 10% from year two.

### Gaps
- Google's exact first-$1M rate for non-subscription items in rollout regions was unclear because the help-page table was garbled.
- The RevenueCat pricing page itself was not fetched, so the method for calculating the 1% is unconfirmed.
